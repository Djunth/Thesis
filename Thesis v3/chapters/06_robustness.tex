\chapter{Robustness}
\label{ch:robustness}

\section{LP}

The headline result in \cref{ch:results} is that High-AI firms exhibit
a negative differential cumulative-return response to contractionary
\texttt{path\_3f} surprises that becomes statistically distinguishable
from zero at $h \approx 30$ and grows through $h = 60$, reaching
$-7.60$~pp at $h = 60$ in the preferred specification. This chapter
asks how much of that conclusion survives reasonable alternatives to
the specification choices that produced it. \Cref{sec:rob-altshocks}
replaces the forward-guidance factor with the target-rate and
balance-sheet factors; \cref{sec:rob-pathinfo} decomposes the path
factor into monetary and information components;
\cref{sec:rob-altai} swaps the strict$+$ML AI classification for the
original broad keyword grouping; \cref{sec:rob-altoutcomes} replaces
the raw cumulative return with two market-adjusted abnormal returns;
\cref{sec:rob-samplewindows} re-estimates the preferred specification
on nine alternative sample windows; and \cref{sec:rob-inference}
checks the inference under four alternative variance estimators and
a wild-cluster bootstrap. Two complementary checks --- a
second-moment outcome and a sign-decomposition of the shock --- are
reported in \cref{app:rob-volatility,app:rob-asymmetry} because they
speak to mechanism rather than to the existence of the headline
differential. Throughout, the outcome, fixed effects, clustering,
scaling, and active sample are held fixed at the preferred-spec
values unless explicitly varied.

\section{Alternative shocks}
\label{sec:rob-altshocks}

The preferred specification uses \texttt{path\_3f}, the
forward-guidance factor of \citet{swanson2021}. A natural concern is
whether the within-sector AI differential identified in
\cref{ch:results} reflects a generic equity response to any FOMC
announcement surprise, in which case \texttt{target\_3f}
(current-meeting target-rate surprise) and \texttt{lsap\_3f}
(large-scale-asset-purchase surprise) should produce comparable AI
differentials. The three factors are nearly orthogonal in the active
event sample (pairwise $|\rho| \leq 0.091$; \cref{tab:shock_corr}),
so differential AI responses are identified from largely independent
variation. This section re-estimates the preferred specification with
each alternative factor in turn, holding outcome, AI grouping, fixed
effects, clustering, and active sample fixed.

Under \texttt{target\_3f}, the High AI differential is not
statistically distinguishable from zero at any key horizon, and the
Moderate AI differential is distinguishable from zero only at the
short $h = 5$ horizon. High AI point estimates
range from $-1.54$~pp at $h = 5$ to $+2.46$~pp at $h = 50$ and switch
sign across horizons; standard errors lie between $0.31$ and
$2.44$~pp. The contrast with \texttt{path\_3f} is in absolute
magnitude rather than precision: at $h = 60$ the path-shock High AI
differential is $-7.60$~pp (s.e.~$2.53$) against a target-shock
counterpart of $+0.65$~pp (s.e.~$2.44$); the path-shock magnitude lies
far outside the target-shock $95\%$ confidence interval at every long
horizon, while the target-shock estimate itself is small and
indistinguishable from zero.

Under \texttt{lsap\_3f}, the AI differentials run opposite in sign
to the path-shock estimates. Point estimates are mostly positive
and grow to $+2.29$~pp (High AI) and $+2.82$~pp (Moderate AI) at
$h = 60$, with standard errors of $1.61$~pp (High AI) and
$0.97$~pp (Moderate AI). The High AI differential is statistically
indistinguishable from zero at every key horizon, while the
Moderate AI differential at $h = 60$ has a $95\%$ confidence
interval of approximately $[+0.92, +4.71]$ that excludes zero. Two
caveats discipline this finding. First, the \texttt{lsap\_3f}
factor is identified primarily during the zero-lower-bound period
(\cref{sec:emp-shock}), so the long-horizon point estimates are
heavily weighted by ZLB-era events. Second, the sign is positive,
opposite to the headline path-shock pattern: even where the
lsap-shock Moderate AI differential is distinguishable from zero
it does not reproduce the negative, monotonically growing
long-horizon profile of the path-shock baseline, but rather points
in the opposite direction.

The headline result is therefore specific to the forward-guidance
factor rather than a feature of FOMC announcement surprises in
general. The differential response is concentrated in news about the
expected policy-rate path, not current target-rate news or
balance-sheet news.

\section{Monetary versus information component of the path shock}
\label{sec:rob-pathinfo}

The high-frequency identification used in the preferred specification
treats every announcement-window revision in the path factor as a
monetary surprise. \citet{nakamuraSteinsson2018} document that such
revisions may embed central-bank information about the economic
outlook, and \citet{jarocinskiKaradi2020} propose to separate the two
by the sign of the contemporaneous equity reaction: when rates and
equities move in opposite directions the announcement is consistent
with a textbook monetary surprise; when they move together it is
more naturally read as a signal about growth or risk. Following
\cref{sec:lp-data}, the path factor is decomposed event by event into
\texttt{path\_monetary} (path $\times$ SPFUT $< 0$) and
\texttt{path\_information} (path $\times$ SPFUT $> 0$), where SPFUT
is the contemporaneous S\&P~500 futures surprise. Of the $128$
scheduled FOMC events in the active sample, $75$ are classified as
monetary and $51$ as information; two with $\texttt{path\_3f} = 0$
are unclassified. The preferred specification is re-estimated with
four interactions in place of the two, one per (component, AI group)
combination. \citet{bauerSwanson2023} offer an alternative reading of
the same sign restriction; the decomposition is reported here as a
sensitivity check rather than a structural claim.

The monetary component reproduces the headline pattern. The High AI
differential under \texttt{path\_monetary} is indistinguishable from
zero through $h = 30$ and turns negative and distinguishable from
zero at $h = 40$ ($-5.91$~pp, s.e.~$2.17$), $h = 50$ ($-5.88$,
s.e.~$2.41$) and $h = 60$ ($-6.01$, s.e.~$2.81$). The corresponding
preferred-spec baseline values are $-6.49$ at $h = 40$, $-7.38$ at
$h = 50$, and $-7.60$ at $h = 60$ in percentage points:
the monetary-component estimates lie within the $68\%$ confidence
interval of the baseline at every key horizon. The Moderate AI
differential under \texttt{path\_monetary} remains imprecise and does
not cross conventional thresholds, consistent with the preferred
specification.

The information component is noisier but, in the updated sample,
statistically informative at the longest horizons. High AI point
estimates are negative at every key horizon $h \geq 30$ and grow in
absolute value to $-13.91$~pp (s.e.~$5.61$) at $h = 60$. Standard
errors are roughly two to three times those of the monetary
component because the information shock is identified off fewer
events. The $95\%$ confidence interval excludes zero at $h = 40$
($-9.06$, s.e.~$4.02$; CI $\approx [-16.95, -1.17]$), $h = 50$
($-13.35$, s.e.~$5.87$; CI $\approx [-24.85, -1.85]$), and $h = 60$
($-13.91$, s.e.~$5.61$; CI $\approx [-24.90, -2.93]$); at the
shorter horizons ($h \leq 30$) the same-signed estimates remain
imprecise and their confidence intervals contain zero.
The Moderate AI differential under \texttt{path\_information} is
small and, apart from an isolated positive estimate at $h = 5$,
indistinguishable from zero at every key horizon, with no
consistent long-horizon pattern.

The qualitative reading is that the monetary component of the path
surprise reproduces the long-horizon differential response of High AI
firms at magnitudes within the confidence band of the preferred
specification, while the information component delivers
same-signed estimates that are statistically distinguishable from
zero at the longest horizons but with much wider confidence
intervals than the monetary component. The criticism by \citet{bauerSwanson2023}, that what looks
like an information effect can also reflect market learning about the
policy rule, applies and is left unresolved.

\section{Alternative AI classifications}
\label{sec:rob-altai}

The headline result rests on a discrete three-way classification of
firms by pre-ChatGPT AI exposure, constructed in
\cref{sec:data-ai-classification} from a combination of a strict
keyword filter and a machine-learning--assisted refinement. This
section asks whether the long-horizon differential survives the
alternative discrete classification that uses only the original broad
keyword set without the ML pass. A continuous standardised log AI
score is considered in \cref{app:rob-altai-continuous}.

The original broad classification (\texttt{high2}, \texttt{moderate2},
\texttt{no2}) is mechanically more inclusive in the High and Moderate
tiers. Of the $450$ firms in the active sample,
\cref{tab:alt_ai_crosstab} shows that the two classifications agree
for $427$ ($94.9\%$) and disagree for $23$: $16$ strict No-AI firms
are reclassified upward under the broad scheme ($3$ to High, $13$ to
Moderate), $7$ strict Moderate firms are reclassified as broad High,
and no firm is reclassified downward.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering\small
\input{outputs/tables/main/lp_strict_vs_original_ai_crosstab}
\end{table}

Re-estimating the preferred specification under the broad
classification yields High AI estimates close to the strict$+$ML
baseline at every key horizon: $-4.20$ vs.\ $-4.21$~pp at $h = 30$;
$-6.49$ vs.\ $-5.68$ at $h = 40$; $-7.60$ vs.\ $-6.95$ at $h = 60$. The $95\%$ confidence intervals overlap completely at
every horizon, and the same set of horizons ($h \geq 30$) exhibits a
differential distinguishable from zero under both classifications.
The Moderate AI estimates differ in level --- the broad scheme
yields smaller coefficients in absolute value, reflecting that some
strict Moderate firms are absorbed into the broad High group --- but
neither produces a Moderate differential distinguishable from zero
at any key horizon. \Cref{tab:alt_ai_key} reports the full set of

The $94.9\%$ firm-level agreement limits what this comparison shows:
the marginal firms reclassified by the ML pass do not carry the
result, but the test cannot speak to how the result would change
under a substantively different exposure measure (e.g., a
revenue-based or patent-based measure). The continuous-exposure check
in \cref{app:rob-altai-continuous} yields same-signed estimates of
comparable magnitude, with an SE-collapse caveat under the preferred
two-way clustering at a subset of horizons that is resolved under
firm-only clustering. The headline is therefore not an artifact of
the specific strict$+$ML construction.

\section{Alternative outcomes: cumulative abnormal returns}
\label{sec:rob-altoutcomes}

The headline outcome \texttt{cum\_ret\_h} is unadjusted for market-wide
return variation. A possible concern is that the AI differential
reflects differential loading on aggregate equity shocks rather than
a genuine cross-sectional response to the forward-guidance surprise.
This section re-estimates the preferred specification with two
market-adjusted alternatives: \texttt{car\_mktadj\_h}, firm return
minus the contemporaneous S\&P~500 return, and \texttt{car\_betaadj\_h},
the beta-adjusted CAR using a firm-specific pre-event market beta.

All three regressions are estimated on the intersection of
observations where the three outcomes are jointly non-missing; the
intersection contains $N = 49{,}863$ firm--event observations at
impact --- below the $53{,}679$ observations in the headline sample
because the two abnormal-return outcomes impose an additional
non-missingness requirement --- and shrinks only mechanically with
the horizon.

outcomes: key-horizon estimates}

\input{outputs/tables/main/lp_car_vs_cumret_key_horizons}

The High AI estimates are nearly identical across outcomes. At
$h = 60$ the differential is $-6.24$~pp under \texttt{cum\_ret\_h},
$-6.22$ under \texttt{car\_mktadj\_h}, and $-4.92$ under
\texttt{car\_betaadj\_h}; at $h = 40$ the three values are $-5.97$,
$-5.97$, and $-5.33$. The beta-adjusted CAR produces slightly smaller
magnitudes at the longest horizons --- consistent with the
adjustment removing some differential market loading --- but the
long-horizon pattern of a statistically distinguishable negative
differential for High AI firms holds under all three outcomes. A
handful of cells in \cref{tab:alt_outcomes_key} are marked ``---''
where the two-way clustered variance estimator collapses numerically
at scattered horizons under the CAR outcomes; firm-only clustering
yields well-defined standard errors at every horizon and leaves the
substantive picture unchanged
(\cref{tab:car_vce_appendix} in \cref{app:additional-results}). The
headline differential is therefore a cross-sectional response to the
forward-guidance surprise rather than a reflection of differential
market loading.

\section{Sample-window sensitivity}
\label{sec:rob-samplewindows}

The active sample covers $128$ scheduled FOMC announcements between
January~2010 and January~2026. Two concerns motivate a check across
alternative windows. The first is whether the headline result is
driven by the post-ChatGPT period (December 2022 onward), during
which AI-exposed firms experienced a sustained rerating that
coincided with the 2022--2024 tightening cycle; a positive
correlation between AI exposure and rerating in that window could
mechanically generate a negative differential. The second is whether
extending the start of the sample back through the post-GFC liftoff,
through the zero-lower-bound era, or back to the full available
history, and either including or excluding the COVID-19 episode and
the 2025--2026 Yahoo Finance extension, materially shifts the
estimates. The preferred specification is re-estimated across eight
sample windows defined in
\cref{tab:samplewindow_definitions}; the first row of the table is
the preferred 2010--2026 sample itself, reported for reference.

The headline pattern survives in every window tested. At $h = 40$
the High AI--No AI differential ranges from $-4.51$~pp (full
1995--2024) to $-7.34$~pp (post-ChatGPT) and is statistically
distinguishable from zero at the $5\%$ level in all ten reported
windows, although the full 1995--2024 (CRSP-only) window is the
weakest case, with a $95\%$ confidence interval ($[-8.82, -0.19]$)
whose upper bound lies just below zero. At
$h = 60$ the range is from $-5.97$~pp (full 1995--2024) to
$-10.63$~pp (post-ChatGPT), again excluding zero in every window
(\cref{tab:samplewindow_key}). The most informative comparison is
the pre/post-ChatGPT split, which spans the AI rerating episode
that motivates the thesis. The post-ChatGPT point estimate at
$h = 60$ is $-10.63$~pp (s.e.~$3.00$) against the new $2010$--$2026$
baseline of $-7.60$~pp (s.e.~$2.53$); the post estimate is roughly
$40\%$ larger in absolute value but rests on only $26$ events, and
the two $95\%$ confidence intervals overlap substantially (post
$[-16.5, -4.8]$; baseline $[-12.6, -2.6]$). The broader pre-ChatGPT robustness window, which runs from 2005 to
the ChatGPT release and excludes the post-2022 AI rerating period,
also yields a negative long-horizon differential. The table
therefore suggests that the headline result is not mechanically
generated only by the post-ChatGPT period, although a strictly
nested 2010-to-ChatGPT window is not separately reported.
The headline is therefore not an artifact of the post-ChatGPT
period. Excluding the COVID year, restricting to
the post-liftoff window, and including or excluding the
2025--2026 Yahoo Finance extension all shift the long-horizon
coefficient by less than $1.65$~pp in absolute value relative to
the new baseline at $h = 60$, and leave every window
distinguishable from zero. The two pre-2005 extensions
attenuate the result by roughly $1.5$~pp at $h = 60$, most plausibly
because the text-based AI-exposure measure is less informative for
the $1995$--$2004$ period during which AI-relevant firm
characteristics had not yet emerged. The Moderate AI estimates
(reported in \cref{tab:samplewindow_key_moderate} in
\cref{app:additional-results}) are indistinguishable from zero at the
seven key horizons in seven of the nine windows; the two exceptions
are the post-GFC windows at $h = 30$, where the Moderate AI estimate
just crosses the $5\%$ threshold with the same sign as the High AI
estimate.

\section{Inference sensitivity}
\label{sec:rob-inference}

All estimates in \cref{ch:results} use two-way clustered standard
errors by \texttt{firm\_id} and \texttt{event\_id}. The
event-cluster dimension is the binding small-cluster concern, with
$n = 128$ FOMC events --- enough for asymptotic inference but small
enough to warrant a check against the wild-cluster bootstrap of
\citet{cameronGelbachMiller2008}. The preferred specification is
re-estimated under four alternative variance estimators, with the
bootstrap reported at the four long horizons where the headline
differential is statistically distinguishable from zero.

Three observations stand out. First, the High AI differential at
$h \in \{30, 40, 50, 60\}$ has a $95\%$ CI that excludes zero under
every scheme. Second, the preferred two-way clustered SE is the
largest of the four standard estimators at $h = 60$: at that
horizon the two-way SE of $2.53$~pp compares to firm-clustered
$2.40$, event-clustered $2.37$, and heteroskedasticity-robust
$2.22$; the same ordering holds at $h = 40$ and $h = 50$. The
preferred specification is therefore not understating its standard
errors relative to standard alternatives. Third, the wild-cluster
bootstrap is computed at the four long horizons where the headline
differential is statistically distinguishable from zero. The
small-cluster correction does not change the qualitative
conclusion at any of the four horizons.

One pattern is worth flagging. At $h = 20$ and $h = 30$ the
event-clustered SE is slightly smaller than the firm-clustered or
robust SE. Clustering normally raises SEs by allowing within-cluster
correlation; the reverse pattern here reflects mild negative
within-event correlation in the residualised returns. The two-way
clustered SE sits between the two single-cluster estimators, as it
should.

\Cref{tab:inference_sens_moderate} in \cref{app:additional-results}
reports the same comparison for the Moderate AI interaction. The
Moderate AI point estimate is indistinguishable from zero at every
key horizon under every estimator. The wild-cluster bootstrap is not
reported for Moderate AI because the qualitative reading is unchanged
under any standard estimator. The inference underlying the headline
result is therefore not sensitive to the choice of variance
estimator.

\section{Summary and remaining concerns}
\label{sec:rob-lp}

The headline result survives the six checks above. It is specific
to the forward-guidance factor: the target-rate differential is
small and indistinguishable from zero at every key horizon, and
the balance-sheet (lsap) differential is opposite-signed --- the
only statistically distinguishable lsap estimate is a \emph{positive}
Moderate-AI coefficient at the longest horizon, identified mainly
during the zero-lower-bound period, which does not reproduce the
negative path-shock profile (\cref{sec:rob-altshocks}). The
headline is concentrated in the monetary partition of the path
factor, with same-signed information-partition estimates that are
distinguishable from zero at $h = 40$ and $h = 60$ but with much
wider confidence intervals than the monetary component
(\cref{sec:rob-pathinfo}). It is nearly identical under a coarser
broad keyword classification (\cref{sec:rob-altai}), preserved
under market-adjusted and beta-adjusted abnormal returns
(\cref{sec:rob-altoutcomes}), present in all nine alternative
sample windows (\cref{sec:rob-samplewindows}), and insensitive to
the choice of variance estimator including under a wild-cluster
bootstrap (\cref{sec:rob-inference}). The heterogeneity analysis in
\cref{sec:res-heterogeneity} establishes separately that the
differential is not explained by financial leverage. Complementary
mechanism-oriented evidence in
\cref{app:rob-volatility,app:rob-asymmetry} is qualitatively
consistent with --- but does not establish --- a discount-rate
reading.

Three concerns the chapter does not resolve are worth flagging.
First, the strict$+$ML and original broad classifications agree on
$94.9\%$ of firms, so \cref{sec:rob-altai} cannot speak to how the
result would change under a substantively different AI exposure
measure (e.g., a revenue-based or patent-based measure). Second, the
preferred specification has Sector$\times$Event fixed effects that
partial out industry-event-level variation but no firm-level
time-varying size control; the High AI group includes many of the
largest S\&P~500 firms by market capitalisation in the late sample,
and a size-loading story cannot be ruled out from the checks reported
here. Third, the chapter does not estimate negative-horizon
coefficients to verify that the High AI differential is zero in the
trading days before the announcement. None of these is a known
failure of the headline; each is an extension the data and pipeline
could in principle accommodate.


\section{Bubbles}

This chapter examines whether the bubble results in \cref{ch:results} are sensitive to three implementation choices in the PSY/GSADF design. \Cref{sec:rob-lags} varies the fixed lag order in the recursive ADF regressions. \Cref{sec:rob-wild} compares the corrected wild-multiplier bootstrap used in the main analysis with the multiplier implementation shipped in \texttt{psymonitor}~0.0.3. \Cref{sec:rob-log} re-estimates the test variables in logs rather than levels. The objective is not to generate a new interpretation, but to test whether the main conclusions depend on lag choice, bootstrap implementation, or the level-form specification. Throughout the chapter, the sample, recursive window rule, control window, bootstrap count, and date-stamping logic follow \cref{subsec:bubble-specification,subsec:bubble-critical-values,subsec:bubble-date-stamping}; only the robustness dimension under review is varied.


% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Lag-order robustness}
\label{sec:rob-lags}

The baseline specification in \cref{ch:results} follows the PSY convention of reporting the empirical application at $k=0$, while \citet[footnote~22]{phillipsShiYu2015a} use $k=3$ as a principal alternative. To examine whether the conclusions are driven by the no-lag specification, the analysis is repeated for $k\in\{1,2,3\}$, with wild-multiplier bootstrap critical values recomputed separately at each lag. The GSADF statistic at a given lag is therefore always compared with the corresponding finite-sample null distribution at that same lag. \Cref{tab:rob-lags-headline} reports the sixteen headline series; the concentration-basket counterpart is reported in \cref{tab:rob-lags-topn}.

The headline evidence is robust in the dimensions that matter for the thesis, but it is not mechanically invariant across every series. Twelve of the sixteen headline series reject the unit-root-with-drift null at the 90\,\% bootstrap critical value at every lag. The four exceptions are all secondary S\&P-normalised diagnostic ratios: the value-weighted Moderate~AI~/~S\&P~500 ratio fails to reject at $k=3$, the value-weighted No~AI~/~S\&P~500 ratio fails at $k\in\{2,3\}$, the equal-weighted Moderate~AI~/~S\&P~500 ratio fails at $k=1$, and the equal-weighted No~AI~/~S\&P~500 ratio fails at $k=3$. By contrast, no High-AI series drops out of the rejection region, and all absolute indices reject at every lag. The lag sensitivity is therefore concentrated in the weaker breadth diagnostics rather than in the High-AI level, High-AI relative, or concentration evidence. This is also the direction predicted by the cross-sectional design: the first verdicts to become insignificant under a more demanding lag specification are the placebo-style and middle-exposure S\&P-normalised diagnostics, not the High-AI or No-AI denominator contrasts that drive the main interpretation.

Two features of \cref{tab:rob-lags-headline} are central. First, the High~AI absolute index remains significant at the 99\,\% level at every $k\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ under both value- and equal-weighting. Moreover, the GSADF statistic rises rather than falls when lags are added: under value-weighting it increases from 4.04 at $k=0$ to 5.21 at $k=2$ and 4.98 at $k=3$, while under equal-weighting it increases from 4.25 at $k=0$ to 5.08 at both $k=2$ and $k=3$. Higher-order autoregressive correction therefore does not weaken the High-AI level rejection. Second, the High~AI~/~No~AI ratio, which is the primary cross-sectional contrast in \cref{subsec:bubble-test-variables}, remains significant under both weightings at every lag: it is classified at the 99\,\% level for $k\in\{0,1,2\}$ and at the 95\,\% level for $k=3$. The exact significance bin is therefore not fully invariant, but the rejection itself is preserved across the full lag range. The one-bin decline at $k=3$ is consistent with the finite-sample efficiency loss that can arise when the recursive ADF regression is over-specified with additional lags in a relatively short minimum window.

The concentration baskets in \cref{tab:rob-lags-topn} show a similar pattern. Eleven of the twelve ratios reject at the 90\,\% level at every lag. The only ratio that drops out is the equal-weighted Top-decile~/~High~AI series, which rejects at $k\in\{0,2\}$ but fails to reject at $k\in\{1,3\}$; this was already the weakest concentration contrast in the baseline table. The Mag-7~/~No~AI ratio remains the largest concentration signal at every lag, especially under equal-weighting, where the GSADF statistic declines from 6.72 at $k=0$ to 3.23 at $k=3$ but stays in the 99\,\% rejection bin throughout. The Mag-7~/~High~AI ratios weaken as lag length increases, but both continue to reject at every lag. Thus the concentration result is robust in its ordering: the strongest evidence remains with Mag-7 and the strongest denominator contrast remains No~AI.

Read together with \cref{sec:results-headline,sec:results-concentration}, the lag exercise supports the main interpretation with an important qualification. The cross-sectional ordering High~AI~$>$~Moderate~AI~$>$~No~AI is preserved across lag choices, and the High~AI~/~No~AI and Mag-7~/~No~AI contrasts remain significant throughout. What changes with lag order is primarily the significance classification of marginal S\&P-normalised breadth diagnostics and the weakest Top-decile~/~High~AI ratio. The lag robustness exercise therefore supports the substantive results, but not the stronger claim that every reported rejection is insensitive to lag choice.

\section{Wild-bootstrap multiplier}
\label{sec:rob-wild}

Inference in the PSY framework depends on bootstrap critical values for the recursive supremum statistic. \citet{harveyEtAl2016} motivate a wild-multiplier bootstrap in which the residual path is perturbed by an i.i.d. mean-zero, unit-variance multiplier sequence. The implementation used in \cref{ch:results} draws an independent $N(0,1)$ multiplier for each observation and bootstrap replication. The implementation shipped with \texttt{psymonitor}~0.0.3 instead draws one $N(0,1)$ scalar per call and recycles it across the multiplier matrix.\footnote{The relevant code is the same in the CRAN build of \texttt{psymonitor}~0.0.3 and in the package's GitHub source, and the issue was reported in the package's tracker in December~2020 without a subsequent patch.} Because the ADF $t$-statistic, and hence the BSADF and GSADF statistics, are invariant to a uniform rescaling of the regression errors, the recycled scalar is absorbed by the statistic. The package routine therefore retains residual-resampling randomness across replications, but it does not implement the observation-specific wild perturbation required by \citet{harveyEtAl2016}.

The comparison in \cref{tab:rob-multiplier} re-estimates all twenty-eight series under both multiplier procedures, holding the data, recursive specification, lag order ($k=0$), control window ($T_b=50$), bootstrap count ($n_{\mathrm{boot}}=\num{2000}$), and seed fixed. The GSADF statistic is identical across the two procedures because it is a statistic of the observed series. Only the bootstrap critical values, and therefore the significance bins, can change.

The result is that the corrected multiplier is conservative relative to the package multiplier in the cases where the classification changes. Twenty of the twenty-eight series have identical significance bins under the two procedures. The remaining eight shift downward by one bin under the corrected multiplier: five move from $^{***}$ to $^{**}$ and three move from $^{**}$ to $^{*}$. No series loses significance entirely. This pattern is consistent with the mechanism above: observation-specific multipliers allow the bootstrap innovation path to vary locally rather than being scaled by a single common draw, so the corrected procedure can generate a wider upper tail in the simulated GSADF distribution. When the corrected bootstrap raises the relevant upper critical value, a statistic that previously sat just above the package threshold may move into the adjacent lower bin.

The affected series are not the core identification contrasts. They are the Moderate~AI absolute index under both weightings, the S\&P~500 absolute benchmark under both weightings, and the Top-5~/~High~AI and Top-decile~/~High~AI ratios under both weightings. The High~AI absolute index, the High~AI~/~No~AI ratio, the High~AI~/~S\&P~500 ratio, all concentration ratios measured against No~AI, and the Mag-7~/~High~AI ratios retain the same significance classification under the corrected multiplier. The bootstrap correction therefore changes some significance labels, but it does not change the cross-sectional interpretation in \cref{ch:results}: the strongest and most thesis-relevant evidence remains located in High-AI outperformance and in mega-cap concentration, not in the secondary middle-exposure or leader-versus-High-AI contrasts.

The implication is methodological as well as substantive. The corrected $N(0,1)$ multiplier is used throughout the main results because it implements the wild bootstrap described in \citet{harveyEtAl2016}. The package multiplier would have reported eight series one significance bin higher in this application. Using the corrected multiplier therefore avoids overstating marginal evidence while leaving the main ranking of series unchanged.

\section{Log-versus-level specification}
\label{sec:rob-log}

The baseline tests in \cref{ch:results} are estimated in levels, following applications such as \citet{baselePhillipsShi2025} and \citet{caspiPhillipsShi2018}. Other foundational applications, including \citet{phillipsWuYu2011} and \citet{phillipsShiYu2015a}, test logged prices or valuation ratios. This distinction matters for total-return indices. The ADF regression includes a drift term, which can absorb a linear trend in the test variable. It does not, however, absorb exponential trend in a level series. A total-return index that grows at a steady compound rate may therefore generate a positive coefficient on the lagged level even when there is no acceleration beyond ordinary compound growth. Mechanically, if a level index grows at a constant rate $g$, then $\Delta y_t \approx g y_{t-1}$, so the recursive ADF regression can read ordinary compounding as a positive coefficient on $y_{t-1}$. In logs, constant compound growth becomes approximately linear in time and is absorbed by the drift. The log specification is therefore a stricter test for acceleration relative to compound trend.

The exercise in \cref{tab:rob-loglevel} re-estimates all twenty-eight test variables after applying $y_t\mapsto\log(y_t)$ and recomputes the corrected wild-multiplier bootstrap critical values under the transformed specification. The sample, lag order ($k=0$), control window ($T_b=50$), bootstrap count ($n_{\mathrm{boot}}=\num{2000}$), and seed are held fixed relative to the baseline.

The absolute indices are the most sensitive to the transformation. All eight absolute total-return index specifications weaken in logs. The High~AI absolute index falls from the 99\,\% bin in levels to the 90\,\% bin in logs under both weightings, with the GSADF statistic declining from 4.04 to 1.44 under value-weighting and from 4.25 to 1.26 under equal-weighting. The value-weighted Moderate~AI absolute index loses significance entirely. The No~AI and S\&P~500 absolute indices also fall from the 95\,\% bin to the 90\,\% bin under both weightings. This is exactly where the level-versus-log distinction should matter most: absolute total-return indices contain strong compound growth, and the log transformation removes much of the component that a level-form ADF regression can otherwise read as explosiveness.

The relative series are more stable. The High~AI~/~No~AI ratio rejects under both specifications and both weighting schemes: it falls from $^{***}$ to $^{**}$ under value-weighting, but remains $^{***}$ under equal-weighting. The High~AI~/~S\&P~500 ratio also remains significant, with $^{***}$ under value-weighting and $^{**}$ under equal-weighting in logs. The Mag-7~/~No~AI and Mag-7~/~High~AI ratios are invariant at the 99\,\% level under both specifications and both weightings. The Top-5~/~No~AI and Top-decile~/~No~AI ratios weaken by one bin under some weightings, but continue to reject. The unstable cases are again marginal leader-versus-High-AI contrasts: the equal-weighted Top-5~/~High~AI ratio loses significance, while the equal-weighted Top-decile~/~High~AI ratio moves upward by one bin.

The log exercise therefore sharpens the interpretation of \cref{ch:results}. The level-form absolute-index rejections should not be read on their own as clean bubble evidence, because much of their strength is tied to compound total-return growth. The relative ratios are the more informative objects for the thesis question, and the key relative ratios survive the more conservative log specification. This supports the empirical strategy in \cref{subsec:bubble-test-variables}: the strongest evidence should be interpreted through High~AI outperformance relative to No~AI and the S\&P~500, and through the concentration ratios, rather than through absolute index levels alone. The date-stamped episodes in \cref{sec:results-episodes} remain baseline level-specification evidence; the log robustness exercise supports the cross-sectional rejection pattern, not a claim that every dated episode is invariant to the transformation.

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:rob-psy}

The robustness exercises support the qualified reading of the bubble results. Under lag-order variation, all High-AI series reject across $k\in\{0,1,2,3\}$, the High~AI~/~No~AI ratio remains significant under both weightings, and the Mag-7~/~No~AI ratio remains the strongest concentration signal. The lag-sensitive failures are concentrated in secondary Moderate~AI~/~S\&P~500, No~AI~/~S\&P~500, and equal-weighted Top-decile~/~High~AI diagnostics. Under multiplier variation, the corrected wild bootstrap lowers the significance bin for eight of twenty-eight series relative to the package implementation, but none loses significance and none of the central High-AI or Mag-7 contrasts is reclassified. Under the log-versus-level specification, absolute total-return indices weaken substantially, while the key relative ratios continue to reject.

The main conclusion is therefore not that every PSY result is numerically invariant. Several marginal diagnostics are sensitive to lag order, multiplier implementation, or transformation. The stronger conclusion is that the thesis-relevant pattern is robust: explosive dynamics are most pronounced in High-AI relative performance and in mega-cap AI-associated concentration, while broad absolute-index evidence must be interpreted cautiously because it is partly driven by compound equity-market growth. This is the same qualified interpretation developed in \cref{sec:results-headline,sec:results-concentration}: the evidence is consistent with bubble-like dynamics around AI-exposed and AI-associated firms, but the robustness checks reinforce that the strongest claim is cross-sectional and relative, not a stand-alone verdict based on absolute price levels.
